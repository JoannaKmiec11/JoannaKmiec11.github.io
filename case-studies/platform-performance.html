Turning "it feels laggy" into a commercial case

Role: Product Manager, Pupil Progress Skills: Evidence gathering, influence without authority, triage design

01The problem

Customers were reporting that the platform felt "laggy." The feedback was broad and hard to replicate. Different users meant different things: tracker loading, mark entry delays, filtering, downloads, calculations or their own network.

The founder was sceptical about investing developer time, and preferred to keep the focus on feature delivery.

02The evidence

I built the case on volume and specificity, not on a feeling, using three separate sources:

  • Intercom, where Support tagged every speed-related conversation, building a growing body of structured evidence over time.
  • Productboard, where performance issues were logged as insights, each with a direct customer comment, name and school.
  • Hotjar session recordings, used to see how speed was affecting what users actually did on the platform.

03What I did

Defined "laggy" properly. I created a triage document for Support and Success to use on calls, guiding them to capture what action, what screen and what device or network context, rather than a generic complaint.

Built a consistent evidence base. I grouped the triage output into themes, so developers received patterns rather than isolated, vague reports.

Made the case to the founder. I worked with developers to understand likely causes per theme, then proposed a targeted approach: don't pause feature delivery, but invest focused time on the highest-impact issues, because speed was becoming a blocker to adoption and trust.

04The outcome

The founder was convinced. Improvements shipped incrementally across sprints: tracker loading, a WebSockets optimisation, faster value calculation, bulk-edit performance, skeleton loading states and clearer connectivity messaging.

3 to 5 a day to 2 to 3 a week Performance-related support contacts, before and after the improvements shipped.

What would not have happened without me

A sceptical founder changed position on where to spend engineering time. That did not happen because I argued harder. It happened because I turned scattered complaints into a pattern no one could wave away.

05What I'd do differently

I would introduce the triage process earlier. Generic "laggy" feedback came in for too long before it was structured. Standardising sooner would have sped up the whole cycle and given the team better data from the start.

Transferable skill: stakeholder influence without authority, building a data-backed case that changed a founder's mind on where to invest.
