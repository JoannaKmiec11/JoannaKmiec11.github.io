Prototyping my way out of the expensive build

Role: Product Manager, Pupil Progress Skills: Problem framing, prototype testing, scope discipline

01The problem

Teachers used the Pupil Progress platform to view students, assessments and Learning Characteristics per subject and academic year. They could enter Learning Characteristic values, but those values were overwritten continuously. There was no way to capture a point-in-time view or compare progress across review points during the year.

Schools wanted to enter and review these values multiple times across the year, not just overwrite a single running number.

02The evidence

  • Productboard insights, each logged with a direct customer comment, the customer's name and their school, so I could see which types of school were most affected and follow up directly.
  • The same request arriving from multiple schools independently, which pointed to a genuine workflow gap rather than a one-off edge case.
  • Support and Success hearing the request consistently and flagging it as a blocker for some customers.

03The decision

The team believed the answer was a bespoke review-point feature. Before committing to that, I weighed it against expanding a workflow users already knew.

OptionProsCons
Bespoke review-point feature Tailored to the exact request Longer to build, more expensive, added platform complexity
Expand the existing snapshots workflow Faster, familiar to users, reuses existing patterns Less bespoke

I ran prototype testing on the more expensive bespoke option first, because that was where the team's conviction was. Testing exposed that the core need, a side-by-side comparison of live data against a point-in-time snapshot, was already solvable with the snapshots workflow we had.

04What I did

I said no to the bespoke build and delivered the simpler solution: teachers could compare live Learning Characteristic and TPG values against snapshots, side by side, on the live tracker view. Same user outcome, far less to build and maintain.

05The outcome

Over a month to one week Choosing the existing workflow over the bespoke build cut delivery time, while still meeting the core need.

The feature was adopted by most active schools shortly after launch, and Support noted it was picked up quickly with minimal friction. I avoided creating a separate new workflow and the platform complexity that would have come with it.

What would not have happened without me

The team would have built the expensive feature. The prototype is what turned conviction into evidence, and the evidence is what justified the simpler, cheaper decision.

06What I'd do differently

I would validate the underlying problem with users before framing it as a new feature. The prototype was useful because it exposed the simpler path, but better discovery questions earlier would have got there faster, without the detour through the expensive option.

Transferable skill: delivery decision-making under scope pressure, choosing the faster, lower-risk path without compromising the user outcome.
